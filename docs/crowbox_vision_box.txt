CROWBOX VISION BOX - ESP32-P4 BOARD + OV5647 CAMERA
Mounting and assembly notes
==========================================================

Updated 2026-10-17: switched from a Raspberry Pi Zero 2 WH + Camera
Module 3 + Coral to a Waveshare ESP32-P4-NANO + OV5647 camera, because
the Zero 2 W was unobtainable. Wiring now matches the Excel parts list
(Cat6 link; the PIR cable and the reward load cell's HX711 land in this
box).


OVERVIEW
--------
The camera, the ESP32-P4-NANO board, a mini buck converter and the
reward load cell's HX711 live in one small clear-lid IP65 box. The box
hangs under the reward chamber, directly above the object pit, with the
clear lid facing DOWN. The camera sits inside the box and looks straight
down through the lid at the pit floor, so the lid works as the camera
window.

The P4 board does the vision work and sends the main ESP32 a short
result string over UART (the 2-wire serial link). There is no Coral:
the classifier runs on the P4 itself.

Three cables enter the box: the Cat6 link to the main junction box
(PG9, back wall), the PIR cable (PG7, front wall) and the reward load
cell's leads (PG7, front wall).


PLACEMENT
---------
- Height: about 300 mm above the pit floor (pit is 160 x 160 mm, 135 mm
  deep).
- Point the camera straight down, no tilt.
- Coverage with the recommended camera (OV5647, 3.6 mm lens, about
  54 x 41 deg): at 300 mm it sees about 300 x 225 mm. The pit's 160 mm
  fits the short side with about 30 mm to spare on each side, so keep
  the camera within ~30 mm of the pit's center along that axis (up to
  ~65 mm along the long axis). Turn the camera so its long axis runs
  the way the box has to be offset.
- Use the sensor's 1280 x 960 binning mode. It reads the whole sensor,
  so it keeps the full view; the pit is then about 680 pixels across,
  far more than the model needs (96-224 px). The 1920 x 1080 mode is a
  center crop with a narrower view. Check the coverage on the bench with
  a ruler at 300 mm before gluing anything.
- Do NOT use Waveshare's RPi Camera (B) (the camera in the P4-NANO
  KIT-A). Its 6 mm lens (43 deg diagonal) sees only about 190 x 140 mm
  at 300 mm, less than the pit.

Why overhead is better than mounting on a pit wall:
- An object looks the same wherever it lands. From an angle, near
  objects look big and far ones look small and squashed.
- The whole floor is one distance away, so all of it is in focus.
- No angle mount is needed.
- It's out of reach of crows and dropped trash.


BOX
---
- Clear-lid IP65 junction box, 115 x 90 x 55 mm (outer), with mounting
  ears (flange) on the base. Example: Anbox model 115A. Generic versions
  sell on Amazon.
- Estimated interior: about 108 x 83 mm floor, 45-48 mm deep. Corner
  screw posts take about 8 mm in each corner. Check the listing for
  real interior dimensions.
- Mounting: the model hangs the box from the two reward crossbars by its
  ears (Excel, Fasteners tab).
- Glands: 2 x PG7 on the front wall (PIR, reward load cell leads),
  1 x PG9 on the back wall (Cat6). Drill the base walls only.
- NEVER drill through the lid. It's the camera window, and any hole
  inside the seal leaks.
- To reach the board (flashing, microSD), unscrew the lid from below.


INTERNAL LAYOUT
---------------
(Box base is up against the reward chamber; lid faces down.)

  ESP32-P4-NANO  on nylon standoffs on the base. 50 x 50 mm. Check the
                 hole size on your board (M2.5 or M3). Leave its USB-C
                 port reachable once the lid is off, for flashing.
  HX711          reward load cell amplifier, beside the P4 board on
                 standoffs. About 34 x 21 mm.
  Mini buck      MP1584EN, 12 V -> 5 V. About 22 x 17 mm.
  OV5647 camera  mounted to the inside of the lid, lens down.
                 25 x 24 mm.

Space budget:
- Floor (about 108 x 83 mm): the P4 board (50 x 50), the HX711 and the
  buck fit side by side with room for cables.
- Depth (about 45-48 mm): the board stack (standoffs + board + header
  connectors) is roughly 20-25 mm from the base, and the camera stack
  roughly 10-12 mm from the lid. That leaves clearance; the old Coral
  depth squeeze is gone.

Before buying, cut cardboard to 108 x 83 mm, lay the parts on it, and
check the cable routing.


CAMERA MOUNTING (inside the lid)
--------------------------------
- Glue four M2 nylon standoffs to the inside of the lid with 5-minute
  epoxy, matching the camera's hole pattern (Pi v1.3-style boards use
  21 x 12.5 mm hole spacing; check yours).
- Standoff length = the lens's height above the board + about 1.5 mm,
  so the lens sits just off the lid. Measure your camera first; the
  standoff kit has several M2 lengths.
- Screw the camera PCB to the standoffs with M2 x 4 mm screws.
- DO NOT let the lens touch the lid; it scratches both surfaces.
- Focus: Pi v1.3-style lenses are fixed-focus, set for about 1 m to
  infinity, so the pit floor at 300 mm may be slightly soft. Test on the
  bench at 300 mm before gluing anything. If it's soft, gently break the
  glue dot on the lens and turn the lens a little counter-clockwise to
  focus closer.
- DO NOT use superglue (CA). Its fumes fog polycarbonate white, right
  where the camera looks through.
- Put a ring of black adhesive foam around the lens, touching the lid,
  to block reflections.
- The camera comes off with the lid. A 15 cm ribbon gives enough slack
  to open it. Disconnect the ribbon if you need the lid fully off.
- Keep the lid's window area clean and scratch-free; it's the lens
  window now.


WIRING
------
Camera to P4 board:
- The camera's own 15-pin ribbon. The P4-NANO's camera connector is the
  standard 15-pin Raspberry Pi type (schematic J3, "15PIN--PI4B"), so
  the cable that comes with a Pi-style camera fits as-is, the same way
  it would on a Pi 4. No Zero cable.

Cat6 link to the main junction box (PG9, back wall; conductor map from
the Excel's Wiring tab, W08; cut both RJ45 plugs off):
  Orange        +12 V           -> mini buck IN+
  White/orange  GND             -> mini buck IN-, P4 board GND
  Green         ESP32 TX        -> P4 board UART RX
                (main GPIO17)
  White/green   P4 board UART TX -> ESP32 RX (main GPIO16)
  Blue          PIR out          -> main ESP32 GPIO34 (passes through)
  White/blue    GND
  Brown         HX711 SCK        -> main ESP32 GPIO25
  White/brown   HX711 DOUT       -> main ESP32 GPIO26

Power inside the box:
- The mini buck makes 5 V from the 12 V. Set it to 5.0-5.1 V with the
  multimeter BEFORE connecting anything. It feeds the P4 board's 5V
  header pin and the PIR (the AM312 runs fine on 5 V).
- The HX711 runs off the P4 board's 3.3V header pin. The board's 3.3 V
  regulator is rated 3 A (schematic), and the HX711 needs a few mA.
- Both boards are 3.3 V logic, so the UART needs no level shifting.

Choosing the P4 board's UART pins:
- Use any two free header GPIOs (the P4 can route UART to any pin).
- Avoid the pins the board already uses (Waveshare wiki examples and
  schematic): GPIO7/8 (I2C), GPIO9-13 and GPIO53 (audio), GPIO28-31,
  34, 35, 49-52 (Ethernet), GPIO39-44 (microSD) and GPIO45 (microSD
  power switch), GPIO37/38 (USB-serial chip), GPIO54 (ESP32-C6 enable).
- Record the two pins you pick in docs/pinout.md.

Flashing:
- The USB-C port does power, flashing and serial. Before plugging a
  laptop in, unplug the buck's 5 V lead from the board's header. The
  laptop then powers the board alone and nothing back-feeds into the
  buck or the Cat6 line.
- Put the glands on faces that point down or sideways, never up, so
  water runs off them.


POWER AND HEAT
--------------
- Waveshare publishes no current figures for the P4-NANO. Measure it on
  arrival with the UM25C USB power meter, in three states: streaming
  the camera, idle, and in deep sleep. Until then, budget about 0.5 A at
  5 V (an estimate). The MP1584EN (3 A) has plenty of headroom.
- Idle draw matters because the buck also powers the PIR, which is the
  wake source, so this box is never fully off. If the board idles high
  (for example, 0.5 W is about 12 Wh a day, ~12% of the battery):
  * Firmware fixes first: hold the onboard ESP32-C6 (Wi-Fi) in reset
    (GPIO54 drives its enable), leave the Ethernet unused, and
    deep-sleep the P4 between visits.
  * Hardware fix if that's not enough: switch the board's 5 V from the
    main ESP32. That needs a control wire; the Cat6's second GND
    (white/blue) is the candidate to repurpose.
- Heat: far less than the Pi + Coral. The shade under the reward
  chamber and hood is enough.


LIGHTING AND CAPTURE
--------------------
- Mount the LED strip around the camera, also pointing down, so light
  is even across the pit floor.
- Diffuse it: a frosted cover, or aim it at a white surface. Bare LEDs
  make glare spots on coins, which confuse the classifier.
- Use a matte, light-colored pit floor (light gray or white), so dark
  objects like beetles stand out.
- Use a camera WITH an IR filter. Without one, daylight coming through
  the deposit window tints the image and shifts with the time of day.
- Take the picture only after the load cell reads steady (the object
  has landed and stopped moving) AND the motion sensor is quiet (the
  crow has stepped back from the pit).
- Combine the result with weight: camera says "coin" + scale says
  5.67 g = quarter. (Quarter 5.67 g, nickel 5.00 g, dime 2.27 g,
  penny 2.5 g, pre-1982 penny 3.11 g, cigarette butt well under 1 g.)
- Training data: save JPEGs to the board's microSD (the P4 has a
  hardware JPEG encoder). Collect 100-300 photos per class (coin, butt,
  beetle, junk) inside the real box under the real lighting.


VISION FIRMWARE NOTES (Phase 6)
-------------------------------
- A separate ESP-IDF project (vision/ in the repo, not yet created).
  Official PlatformIO doesn't support the ESP32-P4; the community
  pioarduino fork does, but native ESP-IDF (idf.py / the VS Code ESP-IDF
  extension) is the safer route. The main firmware stays on PlatformIO.
- ESP-IDF v5.5.3 or later (Waveshare recommends v5.5.4).
- Chip revision: v1.x and v3.x ESP32-P4 chips need different builds.
  esptool prints the revision when it connects ("Chip is ESP32-P4
  (revision vX.Y)"). For v3.x keep the default; for v1.x set
  CONFIG_ESP32P4_SELECTS_REV_LESS_V3=y. Never reuse an sdkconfig or a
  build folder between the two.
- Camera: Espressif's esp-video + esp_cam_sensor components support the
  OV5647 over MIPI-CSI. Its modes: 800x1280, 800x640, 800x800 (RAW8,
  50 fps), 1920x1080 (RAW10, 30 fps), 1280x960 binning (RAW10, 45 fps).
  Use 1280x960. The P4's image processor handles RAW-to-RGB/YUV.
- Model: ESP-DL, int8. Espressif's MobileNetV2 example runs in about
  177 ms on one P4 core.
- Placeholder until a model exists: threshold against the light pit
  floor, find blobs, score roundness, combine with weight. There's no
  OpenCV on the P4; src/cv.py (OpenCV Hough circles) was the Pi-side
  version and won't run here.


MAINTENANCE
-----------
- No Wi-Fi in normal operation (onboard ESP32-C6 held in reset).
- Firmware updates: open the lid, unplug the buck's 5 V lead from the
  board, connect a laptop over USB-C, flash.
- Training photos: pull the microSD.
